Everything finished on GitHub but not in the Modrinth download yet. It all ships with the next release.

Unreleased This page lists what is in PvpBot's source but not in the build on Modrinth. The rest of the wiki describes the Modrinth build. 98 new settings, a dozen new commands and a menu for nearly everything. Every existing setting keeps its meaning. One command leaves: /pvpbot tp rift (see Leaving PvpBot). Most of the new behaviour is on by default, and the new techniques start off.

In short: difficulty is no longer one slider, bots aim and land hits the way a player's crosshair does, and they read you, space, bait and tilt like people. They know 27 mace and spear techniques, regear from an ender chest, march in formations wearing faction uniforms, and fight in armies that hold or break. And there's a menu for every setting.

Difficulty knobs and presets#

skill is now split into one knob per part of it. That way you can build a bot that aims perfectly but reacts slowly, or one that never misses a crit but can't clutch. A knob at -1 follows skill, so a config that only sets skill plays as it did.

Six presets write every knob at once: easy, medium, hard, expert, professional and perfect. Every setting now resolves per bot first, then per faction, then globally. A bot's own values are kept by name in bot-settings.yml, so they survive respawns and restarts.

/pvpbot difficulty [list]
/pvpbot difficulty preset <easy|medium|hard|expert|professional|perfect>
/pvpbot difficulty <knob> <value>
/pvpbot difficulty bot <bot> [list|preset <level>|clear|<knob> <value>]
/pvpbot difficulty faction <faction> [list|preset <level>|clear|<knob> <value>]

Playstyles#

A playstyle sets what a bot wants, on top of how well it plays: when it commits, heals, runs and chases.

  • aggressive commits and stays in longer.
  • safe heals and pearls out early.
  • defensive stands its ground: it blocks more, punishes your mistakes, never chases past a few blocks (it stops, faces you and lets you come back), never backs off to reset, and heals where it stands.
  • adaptive swings between aggressive and safe with the health race.
  • balanced sits in the middle, and default plays as bots always have.

Set it per bot (kept by name), per faction or globally. randomplaystyle rolls each bot its own, by weights you choose.

/pvpbot playstyle list
/pvpbot playstyle <style> <bots>
/pvpbot playstyle clear <bots>
/pvpbot playstyle faction <faction> <style|clear>
/pvpbot randomplaystyle <bots>
/pvpbot randomplaystylechances [list|reset|<style> <weight>]

The opening is new too. A bot walks a new opponent down the way a player does: drifting side to side, pausing to watch, arcing in over the last few blocks and only sprinting near the end. That careful walk is only the last stretch: from further off it sprints and hops in like anyone crossing open ground. From the first hit either way, it fights as always.

The settings menu#

/pvpbot settings opens a paged menu of every setting, generated from the same list the commands validate against, so nothing is ever missing from it.

  • Left-click toggles or steps a value up, right-click steps it down, and shift steps by ten. Q takes a faction's or bot's own value off.
  • Filter by group, search by word, and nudge the whole difficulty a preset up or down from the bottom row.
  • It works on the Spigot edition too.
/pvpbot settings                                 the menu
/pvpbot settings search <word>                   the menu, only what matches
/pvpbot settings bot <bot> | faction <faction>   one bot's or one faction's values
/pvpbot settings list [group|word]               printed in chat instead
/pvpbot faction settings <faction> [word]        the faction's screen, with what it changes marked

Menus and the kit editor#

/pvpbot menu opens a hub of menus, and every button runs the matching /pvpbot command, so permissions and replies are the same as typing it.

  • Bots: a head for each bot. Its panel opens its bag or ender chest live, gives it a kit, restocks it, sets its faction, sends it to follow or fight, pauses or removes it.
  • Kits: edit, wear, hand out, delete, or make a new one (named in chat).
  • Factions: members, kit odds, recruit bots nearby, follow, formation, war, peace and alliance, uniform, disband.
  • Presets, settings and techniques.

The kit editor lays a kit out like an inventory (bag, hotbar, armour, off-hand), with a second page for the kit's ender chest. Kits now keep every item component, so things like shield banners are no longer lost.

/pvpbot menu
/pvpbot kit edit <name>
/pvpbot kit rename <old> <new>      faction kit tables follow the new name

Aim that decides hits#

A swing now lands where the crosshair is, the way a player's client picks what it hits, instead of "in reach, then a dice roll". The bot:

  • sees you a moment late (the tracking knob);
  • flicks onto you like a hand: a touch of overshoot, then it settles;
  • carries a small tremor (aim) and aims at the upper body.

It holds a ready swing until its crosshair is on you, for as long as its patience lasts. Walking a straight line or circling steadily gets you hit; a juke inside its reaction time makes it whiff. A whiff costs it the attack meter, as it does a player.

aim-height7030 – 95Where on the body it aims, percent of height from the feet - each bot a little higher or lower

Senses#

  • View cone: someone behind a bot isn't seen until they're heard or they hit it.
  • Hearing: sprinting (walking at half the range, crouching silent), fighting, a totem going off, eating and pearls make it stop and turn to look. A pursuer it is already fighting is tracked by ear.
  • Blindness and Darkness blind it: it only notices someone within touching distance, though it remembers who it was fighting.
SettingDefaultValuesWhat it does

Fighting smarter#

  • Hotbar only: in a fight it uses its hotbar and off-hand. Anything in the bag means opening the inventory: it stands still, can't swing, and moves the item over. Between fights it refills its hotbar the way its kit laid it out.
  • Damage model: it ranks weapons by the damage they do through your armour (toughness, Breach, Protection, Resistance, crits) and times heals by how fast it's losing health. It backs off sooner when losing the trade and stays in when winning.
  • Reads you: how often you shield, jump-reset and strafe each way, when you pearl, how many totems and gapples you use. A shield player gets the axe every time, a jump-resetter gets crits, a pearl gets followed, and low with no totem showing means all in.
  • Shield game: it drops its shield to a charged axe instead of feeding it, goes all out while yours is down, and keeps out of reach while its own is.
  • Edges: it walks round to knock you toward the void, lava or a drop, and keeps its own back off edges.
  • UHC tools (off by default, because they change blocks): lava at your feet taken straight back, fire under you, a couple of blocks to hide behind while it heals.

Playing like a person#

  • Personality: every bot gets its own habits from its name: how jumpy it is, how wide it strafes, the range it likes, rush or turtle, sprint-comboer or crit-hunter. Weak bots sometimes spam-click air, and pay for it.
  • Spacing and whiffs: out of your reach while its meter refills, back in as it fills. It hovers at the edge of your reach to bait a swing, and punishes a whiff with a free hit.
  • Tempo: it pushes after landing two in a row, backs off to reset after taking two, and takes a breath after a heavy trade.
  • Items in hand first: gapples, potions and pearls come up a moment before use, and now and then the wrong slot comes up first.
  • Panic or clutch: at three hearts a bot either locks in or falls apart. Better bots lock in more often.
  • After a kill: some crouch over the body; most pick the totems, pearls, gapples and potions out of the drop.
  • Fidgeting: standing about, bots look round, glance at people walking past, flick their hotbar and hop.

27 mace and spear techniques#

Difficulty is how well a bot plays; techniques are which moves it knows at all. Each technique is an ordinary setting. The ones bots already used start on, the new ones start off.

  • Core swaps: mace swap, spear-mace swap, breach swap, spear reach swap.
  • Fundamentals: wind-charge mace, crouch boost, cooldown discipline (wind charges now carry vanilla's item cooldown), hit preference, hotbar shield tracking, elytra wind-charge boost.
  • Movement: spear dash (a held charge and the spear on one click, distance picked by pitch), dash chain, spear-macing, wind-charge reset and self-reset.
  • Anti-shield: ground and elytra backstab, stun slam (one-tick, easy or optimal), spear stun slam, shield drain.
  • Finishers: detap spear-mace, detap breach, spear double pop, wind-charge cancel.
  • Rocket mace with four climb-and-drop patterns, elytra spear, and a choice of pearl catch for the pearl launch.

Survival, webs and escapes#

  • Backing off to heal follows the open ground, not a straight line away. The bot looks round for the most open way out that still leads away from you (never into a wall or off a drop), sprint-jumps along it while there's room, and stops about ten blocks off. Cornered, it turns and fights instead of pressing into the wall. While a gapple or potion is up it eases back facing you, the way a player eats.
  • Advanced healing: it buys a gap before it heals (a knockback hit, a web on you, or a short pearl) instead of eating in your face. healing: old brings back the previous behaviour.
  • Off-hand preference (auto, totem or shield), a totem in the main hand when a smash is about to land, answers to being combo'd, and eating at three hunger bars.
  • Webs are held out, looked at and swung, not a block appearing in a sword hand. They can predict where you walk, web your head against a wall, or knock you back and web where you land.
  • Cutting out of a web: shears first (keeping the web), then water, a pearl, then a sword.
  • Escapes: chorus fruit when webbed and boxed in, and water on its own feet when burning.

Regearing#

A bot regears from its ender chest the way it's done on an SMP: it pearls away, sets the chest down beside it, takes what it's short of (loose, or out of a shulker inside) and packs it all back. It waits for room first and recovers everything if you catch it mid-way. A shulker box in its bag works too, with no ender chest needed. It can also restock from a shulker box placed nearby, or mine yours out from under you.

Every step is played out by hand, so you can watch it: the chest or box comes up in its hand and is placed with a swing and its sound, opened with the lid moving, emptied one stack at a time, closed, and mined with its best pickaxe over the real break time (everyone near sees the crack), and then the drop is picked up. How fast it goes follows skill.

/pvpbot restock <bot|all|faction <name>> [instant]   regear now
/pvpbot view inventory|enderchest <bot>             a bot's bag or ender chest, live and editable

Formations, marches and uniforms#

  • Formations: behind, column, line, rows, triangle, wall and open_circle turn with the leader; square, circle, hexagon and grid line up with the world and hold their shape on arrival. Bots carrying an elytra form their own rank at the back.

Team play and armies#

  • Team play: focus fire on the enemy allies are on and the weakest one; peeling off to save a nearly dead ally; splash heals thrown at allies; regearing one at a time.
  • Reaction ripple: orders and new targets reach each bot after its own moment, nearest first, so a side doesn't turn on one tick.
  • Pile-on cap: no more than three on one enemy; the rest take the next or wait at the edge.
  • Skill spread: each bot's skill sits a little above or below its faction's.
  • Morale: a side that is outnumbered and losing members breaks, the least brave first. A leader close by holds them and rallies the ones that ran.
  • Roles: archers hang back, pearl carriers flank, healers stay with the hurt.
  • Loose formations and ally care: slots wobble, stragglers sprint-jump to catch up, and nobody shoots or sweeps through an ally.

Arena and debug view#

/pvpbot arena spawns two bots where you stand and has them fight round after round. It then reports per side: wins, time to kill, hits and damage per hit, aimed swings that connected, crits, smashes, best combo, totems popped, heals, and mid-fight inventory opens. Change a setting, run it again, compare.

/pvpbot debugview floats a line over a bot showing which part of its AI is in charge, its target and how the trade looks, what it has read about you, its aim, and its latest call ("whiffed", "their shield is down: all out").

/pvpbot arena start <kitA> <kitB> [rounds] [skillA] [skillB]
/pvpbot arena stop | report
/pvpbot debugview <bot|all>

/pvpbot diagnose works on players#

/pvpbot diagnose <bot> explains why a bot can't fight. The new form answers the opposite question: why bots can't hurt a particular player.

/pvpbot diagnose <player> [reset]

It reads the player's state the way the game's damage checks see it: game mode, the invulnerable flag, the "client still loading" protection, hit invulnerability, a raised shield, a shared scoreboard team, PvP in the world, and the client version. It lists every bot within 48 blocks, with whether it would fight them and whether it can see them.

From server start, every bot hit aimed at a real player is logged, and the report shows what became of each one:

OutcomeWhat it means
Turned awayThe game refused the hit before any plugin saw it. The reason is checked on the spot.
CancelledA plugin stopped it, and the report says at which event priority.
ShieldTheir raised shield took it.
ZeroedIt went through with no damage left.
LandedIt hurt them.

It ends with a one-line verdict. reset starts a fresh log.

Changed behaviour#

  • Sword crits: the cooldown trick swung at 84.8% charge, which loses vanilla's crit, sprint knockback and sweep (they need 90%). Bots now swing at 92%, so they combo properly.
  • Fewer fake whiffs: strafing used to add up to 40 points of miss chance; it's capped at 15.
  • Shield as a read: the shield goes up only when you can actually hit right now, not in every gap between the bot's own swings.
  • Mace defence: alone under a mace player, a bot holds its ground near the landing spot ready to shield, and a dodge is a late sidestep. Wind-charge cancels are rare: 10% of launches, never twice in 15 seconds.
  • Spear-macers actually smash: the spear dash now solves the velocity that lands on the target, and the smash is always swung with the mace.
  • The opening walk-in ends once the gap has been growing for half a second, so a bot chases someone running.
  • Followers keep up: a formation no longer falls behind once the leader moves. A follower more than a few blocks off its spot sprints back; close to it, it walks at full speed while the leader walks; it only eases in when the leader stops.
  • Looks at what it uses: a bot looks at the block before it places water or a block, scoops water back, or breaks something, and its arm swings like a click. Water clutches no longer happen looking straight ahead.
  • Elytra mace without rockets builds a real smash: it glides toward you, then closes its wings and drops once a fall would land on you, so every block counts. A Wind Burst hit throws it up for another go (three at most). With rockets it makes two passes a flight at most and rests 9 to 14 seconds after one.
  • Cart PvP no longer stands there with a drawn bow. The bow only comes up once there's a spot that works. When shooting first would hurt it too much it sets and pops: rail and cart down next to you, out of its own blast, then a quick arrow while you're still on it.

Leaving PvpBot#

/pvpbot tp rift is no longer part of PvpBot. tp portal and tp pearl stay.

For plugin developers#

An add-on API, BotAddons, lets another plugin teach bots to use its own gear and ways of getting about: guns, grenades, medical items, special armour, a lift or a launcher. With no add-on installed nothing changes. An add-on implements BotAddon (every hook defaults to "not mine") and is asked at fixed points in every bot's tick:

HookWhen
overrideRight after the target is chosen, ahead of eating, pearls and retreats.
fightWith a target, ahead of every built-in attack. Returning false falls through to PvpBot's own play.
idleWith no target, ahead of guarding, escorting and wandering.
routeWhenever a bot walks somewhere, ahead of its own pathfinding: a transport the add-on runs.
blocksSightWhenever a bot looks for a target: something only the add-on knows about (a smoke cloud) can hide a player.

holdsHand, reserved and armourScore keep PvpBot's own auto-equip, bow code and armour picker out of the add-on's way. Add-ons are asked in the order they registered, the first to claim something has it, and a plugin's add-ons are dropped when it is disabled. The hooks run on the main thread for every bot every tick, so keep them cheap.

BotAddons.register(plugin, new BotAddon() {
    @Override
    public boolean fight(Player bot, Entity target, BotSettings settings) {
        return false;   // fight with your gear, or hand the tick back
    }
});
